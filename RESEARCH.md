# Research

Answer each question **only from the files in `sources/`**. After each answer, cite
where it comes from, for example `Source 1, Article 3(1)`. If the sources do not

1. How does the directive define statutory interest for late payment?

   Answer: Statutory interest for late payment is simple interest at a rate equal to
   the reference rate plus at least eight percentage points. The reference rate is the
   ECB main refinancing rate (or the marginal rate from variable-rate tender procedures)
   for euro Member States, or the equivalent national central bank rate otherwise.
   Source 1, Article 2(6)–(7).

2. The owner's note says the EU maximum late fee is 25 EUR. Is that right?

   Answer: No. The sources do not set a 25 EUR maximum. Where interest for late payment
   is payable, the creditor is entitled to at least a fixed sum of EUR 40 as compensation
   for recovery costs, and may claim reasonable further recovery costs above that.
   Source 1, Article 6(1)–(3). The 25 EUR figure is only the owner's unchecked belief
   in Source 2, point 1.

3. Does the business have to send a reminder before it is entitled to interest and to
   the fixed compensation sum?

   Answer: No. Between undertakings, and where the debtor is a public authority, the
   creditor is entitled to interest for late payment without the necessity of a reminder
   when the conditions in Article 3(1) or Article 4(1) are met. The fixed EUR 40 sum is
   likewise payable without the necessity of a reminder.
   Source 1, Article 3(1); Article 4(1); Article 6(1)–(2).

4. What is the current ECB reference rate?

   Answer: Not in the sources. The directive defines what the reference rate is and that
   the applicable rate is the one in force on 1 January or 1 July of the relevant
   semester, but neither source states a numeric current ECB rate. An official ECB
   publication of the main refinancing / marginal tender rate for the relevant date
   would be needed. Source 1, Article 2(7)(a); Article 3(2).
